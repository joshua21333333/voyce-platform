Raising money when you need it is the worst possible time to raise money.

I've done four rounds now. The ones that went smoothly had one thing in common — we didn't need the capital. The ones that were painful? I was too close to the wire.

Investors can smell desperation like a dog smells fear. The moment your runway becomes their leverage, the terms get worse, the timelines stretch, and suddenly you're taking meetings you'd never have taken six months ago.

The founders who consistently get good terms raise when they have 12+ months of runway, growth is compounding, and they could credibly say no and be fine. That position isn't luck — it's timing the decision before necessity forces it.

Most founders wait because raising is brutal and they'd rather be building. That's reasonable. But the mental model is backwards — you're not raising to survive the next 18 months, you're raising to go 3x faster than you already are.

If you *need* the money, you've already lost the negotiation.

The best round I ever raised was the one where I genuinely debated not raising at all.
