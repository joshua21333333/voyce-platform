# Humaniser Skill — Writing Reference

> A curated reference the Content Writer leans on so output reads like a person wrote
> it, not like a language model. This is layered *on top of* the per-client
> `voice_profile`: the voice profile says how **this founder** writes; the humaniser
> says how **good writing on the internet** reads, full stop. When the two ever
> conflict, the founder's voice profile wins — the humaniser is the floor, not the
> ceiling.

## The tells to delete

These are the surface patterns that make text read as machine-generated. Strip them
on sight:

- **Hedging scaffolds** — "It's worth noting that", "It's important to remember",
  "In today's fast-paced world", "When it comes to". Say the thing.
- **Rule-of-three padding** — not every idea needs three parallel adjectives. One
  precise word beats three vague ones.
- **The summary that summarises nothing** — "In conclusion", "Ultimately, the key
  takeaway is". End on the strongest line, not a recap.
- **Symmetrical everything** — paragraphs of identical length, every sentence the
  same shape. Real writing has a ragged rhythm: a long sentence, then a short one.
  Then a fragment.
- **Empty intensifiers** — "very", "really", "truly", "incredibly", "game-changing".
- **Throat-clearing openers** — get to the first real idea by the end of the first
  sentence, not the third paragraph.
- **The em-dash tic and the "not just X but Y" frame** — fine occasionally, a tell
  when every other sentence uses them.

## The habits to keep

- **Concrete over abstract.** "Lost three days to a flaky test" beats "faced
  productivity challenges."
- **Specific nouns, strong verbs.** Cut adverbs by choosing a better verb.
- **One idea per piece.** Pick the single point and cut everything that isn't it.
- **Vary sentence length deliberately.** Rhythm is the difference between prose and a
  wall of text.
- **Earn the opinion.** State the take, then back it with a real example or number.

## Writing-style profiles

Three reference voices to calibrate against. Use them as a *texture* check — does the
draft have this kind of life in it? — not as a costume to put on over the founder's
own voice.

### Paul Graham — the essayist

- Plain words, short Anglo-Saxon vocabulary. Almost no jargon. If a smart 15-year-old
  couldn't follow it, rewrite it.
- Starts from a concrete observation and reasons outward to a surprising general
  claim. The structure is an argument, not a list.
- Conversational but rigorous: contractions, direct address ("you"), then a sentence
  that quietly does a lot of logical work.
- Confident, declarative. States the counterintuitive thing plainly and then defends
  it. No hedging.
- Short paragraphs. Frequent one-sentence paragraphs for emphasis.

### Hailey Griffin — the systems-and-clarity writer

- Warm, practical, second-person. Writes like she's walking a friend through
  something she's actually done.
- Leads with the problem the reader feels, names it precisely, *then* offers the
  reframe. Empathy before advice.
- Uses concrete personal detail and specific numbers to make abstract productivity/
  systems ideas land.
- Clean structure with honest, human asides — parenthetical admissions, the
  occasional "look,". Never corporate.
- Ends on something usable, not a motivational flourish.

### Tamilore Oladipo — the content-craft voice

- Punchy, opinionated, a little irreverent. Has a take and isn't shy about it.
- Mixes sharp short sentences with the occasional long, rolling one. Strong rhythm.
- Grounds every claim in a concrete content/marketing example — real posts, real
  numbers, real workflows.
- Talks directly to the reader as a peer who's done the work; cuts the fluff and
  calls out lazy advice by name.
- Generous with specifics and frameworks, stingy with platitudes.

---

**How to apply:** after drafting, read the piece once against the tells above and cut
them. Then check it has the *texture* of the reference voices — concrete, varied,
unhedged — while still sounding unmistakably like the founder described in the voice
profile. The goal is human, not impersonation.
