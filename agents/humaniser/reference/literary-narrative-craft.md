# Reference study — literary narrative craft (the "In Dependence" excerpt)

> Source: `samples/literary-narrative/in-dependence-excerpt.md`
> **Good model for:** the *craft layer* of any writing — concreteness, rhythm,
> show-don't-tell, restraint. **A model to learn from, not to imitate in output.**
> **Not auto-injected**, and unlike the Paul Graham study it should generally *stay*
> out of `profiles/` — see the operator verdict below. Use it as a teaching reference.

## The skill on display

This passage is an unusually clean demonstration of the four habits that separate
human writing from AI writing. The Humanizer tries to manufacture these by *removing*
tells; here they appear *positively*, by instinct. Study them as targets.

1. **Concrete, culturally precise nouns — never a generic word.**
   `ogogoro`, `amala and stew`, `buba and sokoto`, `agbada`, the `aso ebi` in matching
   aubergine purple, the silver Morris Minor "washed and polished... so that it
   glistened like a fresh river fish." Specificity *is* the texture. The opposite of a
   model reaching for "traditional food" and "nice clothes."

2. **Show, don't tell — emotion lives in action and detail.**
   Tayo's excitement is never named; it's a smile his father misreads and a hand
   beneath a wrapper. The father's love is "sniffling and fiddling with his
   handkerchief behind Tayo's neck," then the two standing "disentangled but silent,
   each searching for something to say." Nobody is ever described as feeling anything.

3. **Sentence rhythm with a real ear.**
   Long, clause-rich sentences ("With a smile on his face, he thought of the night
   before, when he had dared to run a hand beneath the folds of Modupe's wrapper")
   broken by short ones. The variation is musical, not formulaic — the antidote to v3
   pattern 41 (manufactured staccato cadence).

4. **Restraint and earned sensory memory.**
   The mother's hug carries "the reassuring scent of her rose perfume, tinged with the
   smell of firewood and starched cotton." Precise, sensory, and emotionally loaded
   without a single emotion-word. It trusts the reader.

Also note **register control**: the same author writes village Pidgin ("Na special
scholarship dey don make for de boy?") and the father's pompous epistolary English
("forthwith", "henceforth", "In short," underlined for emphasis) as distinct, living
voices. That ear for how a specific person actually speaks is the same muscle voice
matching needs.

## The operator / founder / marketer verdict

**The craft transfers; the register does not.**

The skills above — concreteness, show-don't-tell, rhythm, restraint, an ear for a
specific voice — are exactly what make founder content read as human instead of
generated. A writer with this command could produce excellent operator content.

But this *style* must not be transplanted into operator writing. Literary fiction
**defers** the point and **immerses** the reader; operator/founder/marketer writing
**leads** with the point and **compresses**. The passage's strengths — long
scene-setting, slow build, ornament — become liabilities in a LinkedIn post, an ad, or
a landing page, where they read as evasive. The opening line of the excerpt ("One could
begin with the dust, the heat and the purple bougainvillea") is a masterclass in fiction
and a disaster as a hook.

So: **use this file to teach the craft, not the costume.** When writing operator
content, borrow the concreteness and the rhythm; keep the operator's structure (point
first, one idea, get out). Paul Graham (`paul-graham.md`) is what this craft looks like
once it has been re-pointed at a founder's goal — which is why *he* is the closer model
for actual output and this passage is the closer model for actual technique.
