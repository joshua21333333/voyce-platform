# Reference study — Paul Graham (essayist)

> Source: `samples/paul-graham/how-to-earn-a-billion-dollars.md`
> **Good model for:** thought-leadership essays, founder long-form, newsletter argument
> pieces. The closest of our references to operator/founder register: literary-grade
> clarity pointed at a practical goal.
> **Not auto-injected** — see `../README.md`. Promote to `profiles/` to inject for
> essay-style content.

## Why he's the gold standard for founder writing

Graham writes the way a smart person *talks* when they're trying to make you genuinely
understand something — not the way a model writes when it's trying to sound smart. He is
almost a clean-room demonstration of the v3 Humanizer rules, observed rather than
enforced.

## Observable behaviours (what to learn)

- **Plain, short, Anglo-Saxon words.** "It's hard, but it's possible." No `leverage`,
  no `landscape`, no `delve`. A bright 15-year-old could follow every sentence.
- **Concrete anecdote → general principle.** He doesn't open with a thesis. He opens
  with a *thing that happened*: a politician's claim, a founder growing 93% a month.
  The principle is *derived* in front of you, not announced.
- **Makes the reader do the work.** He has you calculate `log(500, 1.93) = 9.45`
  yourself. The insight lands because you produced it, not because he asserted it.
- **Specific numbers, real names.** 6500 companies, 93% a month, Justin.TV becoming
  Twitch. Never "studies show", never "many founders".
- **Declarative, unhedged, willing to be counterintuitive.** "The way to get the best
  startup ideas is not to look for startup ideas." Stated flat, then defended.
- **Repeats the right word on purpose.** "Growth rate" recurs a dozen times. He does
  not synonym-cycle to "expansion velocity" for variety (v3 pattern 11).
- **Analogies grounded in the physical world.** A skating coach hearing that a triple
  axel is impossible. Concrete, not abstract.
- **Short paragraphs; frequent one-sentence paragraphs** for emphasis and pace.
- **The last line is the sharpest line, not a recap.** He closes on the actual
  mechanism ("it will happen automatically if you just keep making customers happy"),
  not "In conclusion."

## What does NOT transfer

His pieces are *long* and built as a slow argument. That structure suits an essay; it
does not suit a LinkedIn hook or ad copy. Take his sentence-level craft and his
anecdote→principle move; leave the essay-length build for essay-length formats.

## One-line calibration

If a draft sounds like it's *performing* intelligence, it's wrong. Graham sounds like
he's *handing* you intelligence. Aim for the second.
